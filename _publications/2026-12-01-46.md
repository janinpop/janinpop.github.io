Differential piston, also known as petaling, is an optical aberration that will affect the Extremely Large Telescope and can seriously compromise its performance if left uncorrected. In this paper, we summarize a series of numerical studies in which residual convolutional neural networks estimate these phase aberrations from focal plane images. The initial approach uses the four images delivered by a Shack-Hartmann wavefront sensor, while a recent extension uses full-pupil defocused images. Temporal averaging is the main driver of robustness against adaptive optics residuals, enabling precisions of a few tens of nanometres in representative simulations, while polychromaticity and realistic detector noise have a secondary impact. Defocused dual-wavelength imaging further allows the five independent piston modes of the six-petal pupil to be estimated simultaneously and extends the capture range beyond the usual -ambiguity. These results support the development of a compact focal plane sensor that could complement the ELT adaptive optics control loops, subject to experimental validation and improved control of the simulation-to-reality gap.
