The drastic increase in the number of satellites in low-earth orbit (LEO) poses unprecedented challenges for time-domain optical and near-infrared astronomy. We present a deep learning pipeline to analyse satellite streaks in short-exposure, single-band images, based on targeted observations of LEO satellites with the global network of Unistellar telescopes. We develop a simulation software that injects realistic streaks onto telescope images, and we train (1) traditional convolutional neural networks (CNN) to classify images including streak candidates among large data sets, (2) fully-convolutional networks (UNet) to conduct the pixel-level reconstruction of satellite streaks. Besides reducing the inference time on large, unbalanced data sets, this approach helps quantify the performance as a function of streak properties, and allows us to optimize the mask reconstructions down to the lowest signal-to-noise ratios. The CNN classifier reaches a true-positive rate (TPR) of nearly 90% on real satellite streaks while keeping the false-positive rate (FPR) below 1%. The UNet also reaches excellent performance for the segmentation of images selected by the CNN. For streaks with a peak S/N above 5, we recover >80% pixels within the ground-truth satellites masks, for a contamination level of maximum 10 false positive pixels per image (FPR per pixel of 0.001%). For 90% of streak pixels recovered by the UNet, the precision of output satellite masks is >96% at (S/N)peak ∈ [5;8], and nearly 100% at (S/N)peak ≥ 8. We develop a separate UNet to optimize the reconstruction of the faintest streaks partially below the background noise levels, including optimization of the architecture, and tests of various pre-processing methods such as image denoising. With this network, the precision at 90% recall increases to 40.7% at (S/N)peak < 5, and we obtain accurate predictions on the streak endpoint coordinates. Moreover, the UNet performs well on deep images from the Subaru 8-m class telescope without any fine-tuning. Overall, the pipeline allows (1) accurate measurements of satellite positions over broad (S/N) ranges, and (2) efficient generation of realistic data sets for neural network retraining, which makes it promising to mitigate the impact of satellite constellations on wide-field astronomical images from different telescopes.
